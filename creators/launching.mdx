---
title: "Launch a market"
description: "Write a proposal people want to vote for, then give them a market they can trade when the story moves."
---

Someone who sees your question on X should want to take a side. Someone who takes the other side should be able to read the rules and know exactly what they bought. Here's how to do both, using the hypothetical Texas market from [Find the next breakout market](/creators/picking-markets).

## Put the question on Aura

Open [Propose Market](https://aura.markets/propose) and choose **Yes / No** for a one-off question. You'll need an available proposal slot, either from your own stake or through a [creator agreement](/programs/creator-agreements). Search Aura once more before you submit. If someone got there first, you already have a market to share.

In **Compose**, write the title the way someone would ask it out loud. Pick a category and a cover image people will recognize in their feed. Put the precise definition and deadline in the rules. Here's what that looks like:

| Field | Example |
| - | - |
| Market question | Will Texas legalize recreational marijuana in 2027? |
| Resolution rules | YES if Texas enacts a state law during 2027 allowing adults 21 and older to possess marijuana for recreational use. Enactment must occur by 11:59 p.m. Central on December 31, 2027. A later effective date still counts. Otherwise, NO. |
| Resolution source | The bill's final action and enacted text on [Texas Legislature Online](https://capitol.texas.gov/). |

**Test one close call.** Say the Texas House passes the bill in June, but the Senate hasn't voted. That's news you can post about while the market is open. If the bill never becomes law by the deadline and no other law qualifies, the result is NO. Try the [resolution rules prompt](/creators/ai-prompts#resolution-rules) to find a close call in your own market.

For related questions that belong on one event page, choose **Multiple options**. Depending on tier, a bundle can include up to 100 related markets under one vote and one slot. For a scheduled game or season award, **Sports** gives you a guided flow. See [Create a market](/markets/creating-markets) for the details.

## Give people a reason to vote for it

Choose **Review proposal**. Read the title and rules one last time. They're final once submitted. Check the deposit and when the [24-hour community vote](/governance/proposal-voting) ends. Will there still be time to trade if the proposal is selected? If so, choose **Submit proposal** and follow the confirmation prompts.

During voting, use **Copy proposal link** on the proposal card. Tell eligible voters why this question deserves a market *now*. For the Texas example, that pitch might be:

> The governor's race is settled. Legalization still has to get through the legislature. Would you trade on whether it happens in 2027? Here's the proposal.

Use the voting window to draft the launch post and pick the next development you'll cover. In the Texas example, that might be the bill's first committee hearing.

## Give the first trader something to trade

Once the market goes live, open it and check what someone can actually buy on YES and NO. If the book is empty, a great post can bring people all the way to a market where nobody can take the other side.

You can place two-sided limit orders at prices you'd be willing to trade. Keep them small enough that you'd be comfortable if they filled. Qualifying orders can also earn [liquidity rewards](/programs/liquidity-rewards). If you'd rather encourage other people to quote, you can sponsor rewards from the **Liquidity rewards** page. If the market resolves early, you can get back the unused balance that hasn't already funded rewards.

**Share the live market link.** Update your pinned proposal post so the next person who finds it can trade. Put the question, the price you actually see, and your take in the launch post. When the first hearing or floor vote happens, you have a reason to post the link again.
