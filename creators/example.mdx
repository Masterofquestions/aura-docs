title: "Example: one market, start to finish"
description: "A creator spots a question in a weekend livestream and earns $460 after promotion in this worked example."
---

Imagine a streamer you follow announces a weekend subathon. They’ll go live at noon on Friday with a countdown on screen. For this event, every paid subscription adds one minute. When the clock hits zero, the stream ends.

Last time, viewers kept the clock running for 30 hours. This time, fans in the comments are promising to keep it going all weekend. You run a small page covering streamers, and you spot a question people can trade. **Will the subathon last 48 hours?** If you take YES, you think they’ll still be live at noon on Sunday.

## Monday — make the market

You check the timer rules in the announcement and verify the previous 30-hour finish in the stream recording. A search on Aura turns up no market on this subathon, so you propose it with your Iron-tier slot. The official broadcast settles it. If that subathon reaches 48 hours, the market resolves YES. If it ends sooner, it resolves NO.

You share the proposal link under your post about the announcement. While the 24-hour vote runs, you record yourself comparing what fans are promising with how long they lasted last time.

## Tuesday — post your take

The proposal passes. Once the market is live, you check what traders are offering on each side and pin the live link. Your first Reel opens with the streamer announcing the subathon. You cut to the moment the clock hit zero last time, then give your take on camera. **“I think chat can get them to Sunday this time. Comment AURA and I’ll send you the market.”**

On Instagram, ManyChat sends the live market link. Viewers who want to make a market can also get the creator Discord and your signup referral link. On X, you post the clip with a direct link so fans can see the odds.

## Friday and Saturday — follow the clock

The announcement Reel got 40,000 views and drove \$1,400 in trading on its first day. When the streamer goes live on Friday, you post a second Reel showing the clock and exactly how long viewers have to keep it running. It gets 12,000 views but drives \$6,000 in trading on its first day. You spent \$40 promoting it and earned \$27 from it that day, so you make the next clip without buying another boost. [See the two posts side by side.](/creators/measuring)

On Saturday the countdown hits 12 seconds. Another streamer gifts 100 subscriptions, adding an hour and 40 minutes in one shot. You post the clip with the latest odds. The streamer who gifted the subs shares it, and people who missed the launch start following the clock and trading on whether it’ll last.

## Sunday — show the result and what you earned

At 11 a.m., the timer hits zero. The broadcast lasted 47 hours, one hour short. You post the final countdown and the market resolves **NO**. You called it wrong. You still earned creator fees whenever someone traded on either side.

By Sunday, trading on your market has generated \$12,500 in fees. People who signed up through your referral link paid \$2,000 of that total.

| Earnings and spending | Amount |
| - | -: |
| Creator rewards at Iron (2.4% of \$12,500, with no split) | \$300 |
| Referral rewards (10% of \$2,000) | \$200 |
| Total earned | \$500 |
| Reel promotion | −\$40 |
| **Kept after promotion** | **\$460** |

That \$460 includes the first-day Reel earnings. Most of it came later, after the clip of the 12-second save reached the other streamer’s viewers.
